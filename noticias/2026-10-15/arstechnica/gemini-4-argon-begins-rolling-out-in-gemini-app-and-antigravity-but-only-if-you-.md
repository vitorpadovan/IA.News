# Gemini 4 Argon begins rolling out in Gemini app and Antigravity, but only if you pay

Google [revealed Gemini 4 Argon](https://arstechnica.com/google/2026/09/google-announces-gemini-4-argon-ai-model-but-you-cant-use-it-yet/) a few weeks ago, saying that it needed to move cautiously toward a full release because of the potential for nefarious use. Google seems to have worked all that out pretty quickly, though. Argon is [beginning its wide rollout today](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/gemini-4-argon-availability/), but it's only coming to paying users at this time, and even those on lower-tier plans won't have full access.

Nothing about Gemini 4 Argon's capabilities has changed. Google still points to the benchmarks it [published](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-argon/#workingharder) late last month as evidence of the model's advanced reasoning and agentic coding capabilities. The model is supposedly trained to refuse malicious instructions, and an automated misalignment monitor can stop processing if it detects an unintended workflow. Google claims Argon needs that because it's so capable.

The company has a few new examples of how Argon has helped inside Google, where it has been used by thousands of Googlers. Argon has reportedly been instrumental in making YouTube's comment system more efficient. Argon identified redundant emoji rendering that, when eliminated, dropped server load by 45 percent. Engineers also turned Gemini 4 loose on the proprietary drone physics simulator used in Project Wing, and the AI found critical bugs that had been in the codebase since 2018. Google also notes that Argon has advanced its robotics program, showing a measurable step increase in embodied reasoning.
